\section{The Retrofit Problem}
Anti-skid (wheel-slide protection) systems are safety-critical components of urban rail vehicles. The system of this case has been in service in European urban rail since the early 1990s; its test-diagnosis module is built around the obsolete Intel MCS-48 family (report, Ch.~1). As the parts become unobtainable, the choice is between replacing the system and a targeted board-level retrofit, which has to show functional equivalence with the original design under contemporary safety standards. The work documents one such retrofit and proposes a verification structure for it, which has not been validated.

\section{Replacement Architecture}
A flash-based \textbf{Actel A3P1000 FPGA} emulates the MCS-48 CPU and I/O logic with the OpenCores \texttt{t48} core, integrated unmodified, and runs the original firmware from an external Flash ROM. An \textbf{STM32F401RET6} on the STM32 Diagnostics Board receives diagnostic frames over an SPI link through four SFH601-3 optocouplers (three SPI lines and a reset line) and an isolated DC/DC converter, and logs them to an SD card with timestamps of its internal real-time clock. The parts of that board on the Base Board side of the barrier share the Base Board's supply. The board design is M.~Leyer's (2023); the report credits the prior work of S.~Ayoub (2014), M.~Leyer (2023) and A.~Dolata, D.~Ostermann and F.~Schiffelers (2023).

\section{Two-Layer Verification Methodology}
Mapped to the verification side of the V-model, with IEEE 1012 as the intended process:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Layer 1, component and interface tests (white-box).} VHDL simulation (ModelSim ME), JTAG and pin checks, and oscilloscope measurements of the watchdog reaction and the frequency sweep. The archived simulations (of the design) and measurements (on the Rev~0.0 prototype) date from 2023, before the rebuild.
  \item \textbf{Layer 2, system-level tests (black-box).} The integrated module on a dedicated test fixture, driven by YAML test cases linked to the fault cases of the legacy manual; the tester checks the displays by hand.
\end{itemize}

\section{State of the Evidence}
Simulation and the fixture runs on the rebuilt unit (2025, no log archived) indicate equivalence of the fault handling. The only measurement against the original board, the sweep of 2023, shows a deviation: the emulation ends at about \SI{850}{\hertz}, the original at about \SI{920}{\hertz}. The evidence does not demonstrate equivalence (report, Sec.~5.1.3). Six integration defects documented during the rebuild did not come to light in a planned run of the method, and no single method found all of them:

The rebuilt unit was observed operating as a whole in early 2026; its diagnostic path was not verified separately, and no log or capture of that run was archived (report, Sec.~5.3, Table~5.4).

\section{Standards and Limits}
The layers are mapped to IEC 61508, EN 50129, EN 50716 and IEEE 1012 as whole standards; the mapping is not an assessment of compliance, and no SIL or integrity level has been assigned (report, Sec.~5.4). Formal methods, structural coverage, accredited environmental testing and full HIL integration were beyond the project's scope.
